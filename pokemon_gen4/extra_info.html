Gen 4 catching strategy

Hit points and statuses

The capture formula is the same as Gen 3 with a few tweaks. In order to have the best chances of a successful capture, a Pokémon's hit points need to be as close to 1 HP as possible and its status Sleeping or Frozen.

The move False Swipe cannot KO a Pokémon, while Spore has a 100% chance to put the Pokémon to sleep. The Parasect and Breloom evolution lines remain the classic options, but Gen 4 also gives access to Gallade and to Smeargle, who can sketch both moves.

Poké Balls

Every Ball has a different multiplier used in the catching formula, with sometimes a condition to fulfill. The standout newcomer of Gen 4 is the Dusk Ball, which matches the Ultra Ball in caves and at night. Here is the list of the most relevant Poké Balls:

Ball Multiplier Condition
Quick Ball x4 to x1 Thrown on the first turn of the encounter (multiplier x1 during turn 11)
Dusk Ball x3.5 In caves or at night (Gen 4 novelty)
Ultra Ball x2 N/A
Net Ball x3 Water or Bug-type Pokémon
Nest Ball x3.9 to x1 Low-level Pokémon (multiplier x2.1 at level 19)
Repeat Ball x3 Pokémon already caught in the Pokédex
Timer Ball x1 to x4 Increases strength every 10 turns (multiplier x2 at turn 30)
Apricorn Balls x1 to x4 HeartGold & SoulSilver only, crafted by Kurt: Fast, Level, Lure, Love, Friend, Moon and Heavy Balls each with their own condition

Transferring from Gen 3 - the Pal Park

Seven Pokémon are absent from every Gen 4 game: Mew, Celebi, Regirock, Regice, Registeel, Jirachi and Deoxys. All of them have to be caught in the Gen 3 games and transferred through the Pal Park, located on Route 221 (unlocked after the National Pokédex).

  • Transferring requires a Game Boy Advance cartridge in the GBA slot of the Nintendo DS, with the Pokémon to migrate stored in the PC. Six Pokémon can be migrated per cartridge every 24 hours.
  • Once migrated, the Pokémon are caught in a special Catching Show in the Pal Park itself, with Park Balls that never fail. Only ONE catch attempt per Pokémon is allowed, so bring a Pokémon with False Swipe and a sleep move for the fastest show score.
  • The Gen 3 guide of this website details how to obtain every one of these seven Pokémon, including the event ones, before transferring them.

Special encounter mechanics

Poké Radar

The Poké Radar is obtained from Professor Rowan after completing the Sinnoh Pokédex. Charging it with 50 steps and using it in tall grass chains encounters of species that are otherwise unobtainable, such as Nidoran♀, Lickitung or Taillow depending on the route. Chaining the same Pokémon also raises shiny odds, which is how most shiny hunters operate in Gen 4.

Honey trees

Slathering a tree with Honey (bought in Floaroma Meadow) attracts a Pokémon after 6 to 24 real-life hours. The 21 honey trees of Sinnoh share most of their species, but four special trees per game can also attract Munchlax.

Daily swarms

Once the National Pokédex is obtained, Dawn's house (Route 201) in Diamond, Pearl and Platinum, or Professor Oak's Pokétch radio in HeartGold and SoulSilver announces a daily swarm: a common route temporarily hosts a rare species. Swarms are the main source of several Gen 1-3 species in Gen 4.

GBA slot encounters

With a Gen 3 cartridge in the DS GBA slot, several Sinnoh routes gain encounter slots dedicated to that game: Growlithe appears on Route 201 with FireRed inserted, Lotad with Sapphire, and so on. Full details are on every route's catch locations table.

Radio songs

In HeartGold and SoulSilver, tuning the radio to the Hoenn Sound or Sinnoh Sound station while walking in tall grass adds temporary encounter slots, bringing species like Skitty, Bagon or Buizel into the Johto and Kanto routes.

Safari Zone objects

The customizable Johto Safari Zone places species according to the area and the number of objects placed in it. The catch locations page lists every requirement: for example Gible needs Rocky Beach objects, and Wobbuffet appears at night with Peak objects.

Event Pokémon

Four Pokémon are only available through events in Gen 4. The legendary Darkrai (Member Card, Newmoon Island), Shaymin (Oak's Letter, Flower Paradise) and Arceus (Azure Flute, Hall of Origin) each require their item distributed over Nintendo WFC. Since the official servers are long gone, private DNS servers that emulate the old distributions are now the standard way to receive the items; the same trick also serves the Ranger Manaphy egg missions. Phione simply hatches from Manaphy eggs, so breeding it with a Ditto covers both entries.

HeartGold and SoulSilver also feature the Sinjoh Ruins: bringing a distributed Arceus to the Ruins of Alph allows creating one level 1 legendary dragon of choice (Dialga, Palkia or Giratina), which is a convenient second copy for trading.

Fossils

In Diamond, Pearl and Platinum, the Oreburgh Mining Museum revives every fossil dug up in the Sinnoh Underground: the Skull and Armor Fossils (Cranidos and Shieldon) plus the five older ones. HeartGold and SoulSilver revive Helix, Dome and Old Amber fossils at the Pewter Museum of Science.
